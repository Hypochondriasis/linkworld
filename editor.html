    Getting started

    Everything you make lives in the link: the levels, their boxes, textures and entities, and the models. There is no server. Copy link gives you a link that plays it, Play test opens it in a new tab, and Open link… loads any Linkworld link back into the editor (Undo brings your previous draft back). Your draft is saved in this browser as you work.

    The counter in the toolbar shows how long the link is. It turns red past the size set in Options (some chat apps cut long links). If it says Invalid level, the reason follows: fix that before sharing.

    Levels

    One link can hold up to 64 levels. + Level adds one, the list switches between them. Exits move the player from one level to another. Name, ambient light, sky / fog color and fog distance of a level are in Options.

    Screens

    Level builds the level, Textures and Particles make what it looks like, Models makes objects to place in it, and Wiki is this page.

    Units

    1 unit is 1/8 of a block (a block is about a meter). The player is 14 units tall and 5 wide; they climb steps of up to 3 units. Y is up, and an angle of 0 faces −z (north, up in the top view).

    Views and moving around

    2D views

    The top view looks down (x right, z down), the side view looks from the front or side (pick one in its corner). Drag empty space or right-drag to pan, use the wheel to zoom. The top view only shows and picks what is in the current layer (Layer y and height in the toolbar, Down / Up move it); the side view shows the layer as dashed lines. Snap sets the grid everything snaps to.

    3D view

    Hold the right mouse button to look around and fly with W A S D, Q / E down / up, Shift to go faster. Left click selects a box. The lighting is the real baked lighting of the game.

    The dividers between the views can be dragged.

    Boxes and faces

    Levels are made of boxes. With the Box tool (B) drag a rectangle in a 2D view: in the top view it fills the current layer, in the side view it takes the depth of the selected box (or one block). With Select (V) drag a box to move it, drag its edges to resize it, or type exact numbers in the panel.

    Solid boxes block the player; others are only seen (grass, bars, decoration).

    Textures on faces

    Pick a texture in the panel to put it on all six faces. To texture one face, choose it in Face, or press Tab for face mode and click the face in the 3D view. Scale is how many units one repeat of the texture covers; Offset shifts it. Textures follow the world, so neighbouring boxes line up.

    Ctrl+D duplicates the box, Del deletes it (and what uses it).

    Shaped boxes (Vertex tool)

    The Vertex tool (E) turns boxes into ramps, wedges and slopes: select a box and drag its corner handles. In the top view a handle moves the corners stacked under it; in the side view you can move one. In 3D, click a corner, then drag one of its arrows (x red, y green, z blue) to move it along that axis.

    Corners only move inwards, and the shape is always the convex hull of the corners. A shape that would be flat is refused. Reset to box undoes the shape. The player walks up shaped boxes like any floor.

    Doors, triggers, buttons, exits

    These use a box: select it and press Make door, trigger or exit. A box can have several roles.

    Doors

    A door slides a distance in a direction, or (Moves: Rotate) turns about one of its edges. It carries a player standing on it, so a door moving up is a lift, and pushes the player out of its way. A door nothing activates opens when the player comes near and closes 2 seconds after they leave.

    Triggers

    A trigger's Fires setting picks what sets it off. When entered: the player walks into it; its box is invisible in the game. When used (E): the player looks at it up close and presses E, seeing its prompt meanwhile. When shot: the player shoots it. Use and shoot triggers keep their box visible and solid, so any part of the level can be a button or a target. A trigger fires every time, or with Only the first time ticked just once (a spent button shows no prompt). Each one lists what it activates: tick doors, messages and emitters in its panel.

    Anything nothing activates runs on its own: doors open when the player is near, messages show and emitters start at level start.

    Exits

    An exit box (invisible in the game) takes the player to another level of this link, to another Linkworld link, or back to the menu.

    Lights, messages, emitters, props

    Place these with their tools by clicking in a 2D view; drag them with Select to move them.

    • Start (P): where the player begins. Drag in the top view to set the direction they face.
    • Light (L): color and radius. Light is baked when the level loads, with shadows from boxes. Normal-mapped textures still respond to it.
    • Message (M): text shown on screen when activated (or at level start).
    • Emitter (F): sends particles of a particle type. Its yaw and pitch set where it fires (the arrow). A continuous type starts and keeps going; a burst type emits once each time it is activated.
    • Prop (O): places a model from the Models screen. Its angle turns it. Props are lit like the level but are never solid; put a box around one if it should block the player. A model's emitter is always on.

    Textures

    Textures are recipes, not images: a list of ops run on a stack of layers. Generators (Noise, Cells, Bricks…) and shapes push a layer, filters change the top one, and combiners (Add, Over, Blend…) merge the top layers. The result is the top layer's color. Start from a preset to see how they are built.

    Drawing

    Pick a Draw tool and drag on the preview to add a shape, composited with Over. Polygon: click the points, then double-click (or Finish polygon). Text: click to place it, drag it to move it, Shift+drag to rotate it.

    Shade and normal maps

    Shade lights the height of the top layer and must be the last op. It also turns on normal mapping in the game, so lights catch the bumps.

    Transparent textures

    A Transparent texture uses the top layer's mask as opacity (glass, grates, leaves, soft particles). It has no normal map.

    Level and shared textures

    The List switch picks which textures you edit: This level (for boxes and the level's particle types) or Shared, the link's own textures used by models and shared particle types. A level can have up to 64 textures and 256 ops in all; so can the shared list.

    Particles

    A particle type is like a texture: make it here, then give it to emitters (or to a model's emitter). Rate is particles per second (for burst types, per activation), life is in tenths of a second, spread 0 fires straight and 255 in all directions, and negative gravity makes them rise (smoke, flames). Additive makes them glow; give them a Transparent texture for soft or round particles.

    Like textures, the List switch picks the level's types or the shared ones. Shared types are for model emitters, which are always on: Burst is ignored there. At most 2048 particles exist at once.

    Models

    Models are objects made of meshes: torches, crates, enemies, guns, projectiles. They belong to the whole link, so every level can use them, and you place them with the Prop tool. A model faces −z (north) and stands on y = 0.

    Base and detail

    A model is built in two stages. Base parts are mirrored across x = 0: build only the +x half and the other half comes free (half the bytes). Detail parts are drawn once, for the little one-sided touches. A part's checkbox switches it between the two; in the views mirrored copies are dashed.

    Parts

    Add a part as a box, cylinder or sphere, then shape it with the modeling tools. Each part is a solid color or a shared texture (Shared textures… opens the Textures screen on the shared list). Texture scale is how many model units one repeat covers, unless the part has its own UVs.

    Scale

    Models use a fine grid of their own: Scale is how many world units 32 model units are, so the default 8 makes 4 model units one world unit. Coordinates go from −255 to 255.

    Emitter

    A model can have one particle emitter, using a shared particle type, always on. Drag its orange marker in the views or type its position; yaw and pitch set where it fires.

    Limits

    Up to 64 models, 32 parts each, 255 vertices and 255 faces per part. The props of one level may draw at most 32768 triangles together (a quad is two, a base part counts twice).

    Modeling tools

    The top, front and side views edit the vertices of the selected part; the 3D view shows the result (drag to orbit, wheel to zoom).

    • Select: click a vertex (vertices stacked under it in that view come too; use another view to pick one), Shift+click to add or remove, drag empty space to box-select, A for all, Esc for none.
    • Move: drag a selected vertex; the whole selection follows, snapped to the Snap grid.
    • Add a vertex: double-click empty space. It goes at the depth of the part's middle.
    • Make face (F): select 3 or 4 vertices. The order doesn't matter; the face points away from the middle of the part. If it points the wrong way, Flip faces.
    • Extrude (E): select every vertex of one or more faces. They move out along their normal by the snap distance and side faces join them to where they were; the new vertices stay selected, so drag them on. Extrude the end of a cylinder for an arm, the top of a box for a step.
    • Merge (M): joins the selected vertices into one at their middle. Faces that collapse lose those corners. Merge the top of a box into a point for a pyramid, or two vertices at one spot to stitch shapes.
    • Delete faces removes faces whose vertices are all selected (to open a hole), Delete vertices (Del) removes vertices and the faces using them.

    Faces are seen from one side only: the outside. A face you can't see from outside in 3D points inwards.

    UV editing

    UVs say which part of the texture each face shows. Until you edit them, a part's faces are projected along their main axis with the part's texture scale, which works well for boxy shapes.

    Switch the bottom-right view to UV. It shows the part's faces over its texture, where 64 units are one repeat of the texture (one texel of a 64 px texture is one unit). With vertices selected it shows only the faces they cover, so select a face or a side to work on it alone.

    Click, box-select and drag UV points like vertices. Points of one vertex at one spot move together; faces that share a vertex but had different UVs there stay apart. The first UV edit gives the part its own UVs (from then on texture scale no longer applies), and new faces get projected ones. Reset UVs projects the shown faces again (with nothing selected, all of them, and the part goes back to projection).

    Mirrored copies of base parts use the same UVs, so their texture is mirrored too.
